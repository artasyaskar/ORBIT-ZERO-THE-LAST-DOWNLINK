# Devpost submission text

## Tagline
Every scan costs power and every cube fights for the last downlink. Triage disaster evidence by hand in VR before your satellite pass ends.

## Description
ORBIT ZERO: THE LAST DOWNLINK puts you in the seat of a satellite operator during the last minutes of an orbital pass over a disaster. Three ground teams need answers. You have one battery, a small onboard memory and a single short contact window with the Karachi ground station. You cannot send everything, so you decide what the people on the ground receive.

The whole game is played seated, with your hands. Point a hand at a glowing sector on the floating globe and pinch to select it. Pick the sensor that can see through that sector's weather, then pinch the scan plunger. A data cube appears in the tray at your left. Lift it to read what the scan saw and how sure it is: CONFIRMED, PROBABLE or UNVERIFIED. Carry it into the amber cradle to compress it, then drop it into the downlink bay, where slot order is transmission order. Pull the lever and watch the evidence stream down while the window closes. If your hand tracking drops while you carry a cube, it returns to its tray, safe and unchanged.

The puzzle comes from real Earth-observation trade-offs, simplified and clearly labelled as simulation. Optical cameras give sharp images but are blocked by cloud and smoke. Radar sees surface shape through cloud and dust but cannot measure temperature, and metal bridges and steep slopes cause glare. Thermal infrared sees heat through wildfire smoke but not through thick storm cloud, and its pixels are coarse. Compression makes a cube smaller and faster to send at an energy cost, and it can never invent detail that a blocked scan did not record.

The campaign has three operations. In Fireline, a wildfire races through a canyon and smoke hides the evacuation road. In Monsoon Shield, a levee breach floods a basin under storm cloud. In Aftershock, an earthquake meets a crippled battery and you cannot answer everyone, so you choose who gets help. A nine-step training pass teaches every move without a clock. Every result shows what reached the ground, what did not, and one concrete way to do better.

The game runs in Meta Quest Browser from a link, with nothing to install. It is built with Meta's Immersive Web SDK, Three.js and spatial UIKit panels, with procedural models and synthesized audio. The scene is tuned for Quest 3: 41–49 draw calls and 29k–34k triangles per eye in play (strictly within a 50-draw / 35k-triangle budget), zero per-frame heap allocations in the hand-tracking hot path, and automatic quality fallback if the frame rate drops. Options include captions, large text, high contrast, reduced motion, dominant-hand selection (right/left), one-handed play and a relaxed clock.

All scenarios are fictional simulations grounded in remote-sensing physics. The game does not control real satellites, use live imagery or predict real rescues.
